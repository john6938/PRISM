## Feedback and revision in academic writing

The present article reports on an intervention that combined written feedback with guided revision. In the context of the study, forty students produced two drafts of an argumentative essay. The quality of the revisions was assessed by two independent raters. It is evident that students who received focused feedback made more substantial changes.

There is a clear relationship between the type of feedback and the depth of revision. As a result of the intervention, many students reported greater confidence in their writing. However, it is possible that the novelty of the task influenced their responses. We may conclude that feedback is most useful when it is specific and timely.

From a pedagogical perspective, I firmly believe that we should distinguish between acquisition and explicit knowledge. A number of scholars have argued that formulaic sequences reduce the processing load of speakers. It is widely accepted that native speakers rely on a large stock of prefabricated phrases.

In the context of the present thesis, formulaic language is defined as a sequence of words that is stored and retrieved as a whole. The nature of the evidence for this view is varied. There is a substantial body of work on corpus frequency, and there is a smaller body of experimental research. ## Measuring lexical diversity in short texts

This paper presents a comparison of lexical diversity measures applied to short learner texts. In the context of the study, we compared four indices across three proficiency levels. The stability of the measures was assessed by resampling. It is clear that some indices are strongly affected by text length.

When interpreting these results, I also believe that we should examine the influence of the learning environment. There is a tendency for classroom texts to repeat the vocabulary of the prompt. As a result of this repetition, diversity scores may underestimate the true range of the learners. The design of the task therefore matters a great deal.

It is likely that segmental measures provide more stable estimates for short texts. We may recommend that researchers report more than one index. In the remainder of the paper, we describe the data and the procedure in detail.

## Collocation errors in spoken learner English

The present paper investigates collocation errors in a small corpus of spoken learner English. In the context of the study, errors were identified by two annotators and classified by type. The majority of the errors involved verb and noun combinations. It is evident that learners often transfer patterns from their first language.

There is a need for teaching materials that focus on collocation. As a result of our findings, we propose a set of short activities for intermediate classes. The results of the pilot were encouraging, although the number of participants was small. It is possible that spoken tasks reveal different problems from written tasks.

## Feedback in second language writing: a critical review

The role of written feedback in second language writing has been debated for several decades. In the context of the review, we examine studies of direct, indirect and metalinguistic feedback. The results of the studies are mixed, and the quality of the designs varies. It is evident that short interventions rarely produce lasting effects.

There is a growing consensus that feedback should be selective. As a result of this consensus, many teachers now focus on a small number of error types. We may suggest that the debate has moved from whether feedback works to how it works. In the final section, we propose a research agenda for the next decade.
